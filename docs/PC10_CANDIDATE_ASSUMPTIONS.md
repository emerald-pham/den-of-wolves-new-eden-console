## Source confirmation

The private reference manifest and provenance inventory were checked against all seven primary component/rule PDFs. Candidate rules were checked visually against Base Set Components v1.1, A4 single-sided, PDF page 34. Endgame guidance was checked visually against the Facilitator Guide, PDF page 21 / printed page 19. The routed exploration/away-mission and facilitation summaries were used to locate those primary pages. The existing PC01-A2 assumption authorizes protected per-request Deep Nebula scout markers. Private source files, extracted pages and wording are not included in this repository.

The component gives the Ring three repair requirements: ten materials, one Engineering Shuttle and five different eligible research tracks at four crosses. Each vessel pays five fuel for passage. A far-side sabotage decision prevents pursuit through the Ring. Nebula attempts use one server die, accumulated scouting at that location and earlier vessel losses there; nine is the success threshold. An unsuccessful ship is lost and helps later attempts. The Station requires eliminating the entire occupying Wolf force, including immediate repeated attacks against survivors. Activation requires at least eighteen available Reactor charge capacity from cannibalised vessels. Initial Station pressure is at least one Battlestation plus twenty damage from other Wolf vessels.

## N/O edition choice

The owner approved Home as the default and Deluxe as a clearly labeled complete alternative. This does not establish author-confirmed precedence between editions and does not choose any separate facilitator policy. Both editions share the Engineering Shuttle requirement, five Ring passages at five fuel per vessel, one protected scouting total for Nebula attempts, and the existing Station rules. The P/Station path continues to use the Base rules regardless of this N/O edition setting.

The Home N/O wording was checked against Home Printing, *DoWNE - A4 Single Sided v1.1.pdf*, PDF page 34, SHA-256 `4e3ce6b500716fe139440d669aeae2e7fb4ab85071c98801f28144305f60f8d3`. Home N requires ten materials and five distinct eligible research tracks with at least four crosses. Home O reaches New Eden on a server d6 plus protected scouting plus earlier losses at that coordinate totaling at least nine; those earlier losses contribute to later attempts. Home includes the printed far-side sabotage option to stop Wolf pursuit through the Ring.

The Deluxe N/O wording was checked against Deluxe Components, *DoWNE - Away Missions v1.1.pdf*, PDF pages 14–15, SHA-256 `cf1a4cfcc84c260cc64d1c4810cbeef1463b54fe8acdcc9d404833d2ec7c0513`. Deluxe N requires fifteen materials and five distinct fully completed Endeavour technologies. A track is complete only at its actual catalogued final research box; different tracks have different lengths. Deluxe O reaches New Eden on a server d6 plus protected scouting totaling at least eight, without adding earlier losses. Deluxe permits far-side sabotage after a paid crossing and records ships left behind, but its face does not say that sabotage stops the Wolf Fleet; only Home explicitly states that effect.

The server stores one session-wide N/O edition in `serverState/candidates`; legacy state without an edition resolves to Home. The GM can change it only before any N or O site has state in any fleet group. Selection uses the shared candidate revision and a durable request receipt, and N/O actions resolve against the stored edition rather than client-supplied choices. An already-started N/O candidate locks the edition across groups. The UI shows the selected edition to members and exposes the selector only to the current GM before that lock.

## Digital choices left open by the printed component

- Contributions use current server inventories, research and craft ownership. Research qualification does not erase lab crosses; it records five distinct eligible tracks for this repair. The contributed Engineering Shuttle is permanently consumed by Ring repair. The private reservation and removal of its docking commit together; it cannot launch, repair, recharge, carry out missions, supply boarding teams or fund another site. This durable source consequence is retained through later parking and Team boundaries. Materials are deducted atomically. Repair completes when the last requirement commits.
- Ring passage is chosen and paid separately by each vessel. The GM makes an explicit far-side sabotage call after at least one paid crossing. Its operational view lists vessels that have not crossed. Sabotage records their left-behind disposition without inventing a population death or arrival. This is a deliberate cutoff policy, not an automatic consequence of the first crossing.
- Nebula scouting and earlier losses are counted at the physical candidate coordinate, including prior losses from another group there. They stay server-private. Each vessel has one durable reached-or-lost result. A lost vessel becomes destroyed, its current survivor ledger becomes zero and its old ship actions close. Replays preserve the original die and cost.
- Separate groups retain their own candidate choices. A group succeeds at the Nebula only after each current group vessel has a reached/lost disposition or was already destroyed, and at least one reached safety. The global session closes only when every active fleet vessel has a disposition or was already destroyed. An all-lost fleet uses the existing total-fleet-loss outcome. Mixed settled groups do not cause a first-vessel fleet-wide success.
- Station liberation is a durable private marker written only by the source-bound Wolf finalizer after its verified receipt has no surviving Wolves and its repeat plan stops. A later unrelated attack cannot erase it. Reactors count their current undamaged/upgraded available capacity once. A cannibalised ship closes its vessel actions while its living survivor ledger remains intact. Activation settles the current group at the Station and changes its operational title to New Eden Station.
- The shared ending helper receives the actual post-consequence survivor aggregate. That aggregate is current population accounting; it does not claim that a living left-behind vessel reached New Eden. Candidate group summaries retain the separate disposition.

## Integration contracts

`readCandidateWorkspace` is an ordinary authenticated callable. A player supplies the current group; a GM additionally supplies an owned live instance. The server returns only discoveries known to that group, costs, requirements, blockers, next actor and group vessel results. Only the GM receives private calculation records. Hidden scout totals, arithmetic and foreign vessel lists are excluded from player replies.

`executeCandidateAction` accepts one exact chosen action, current group, candidate identity, expected candidate revision and request ID, plus only that action's human choices. It accepts no dice or calculated outcome. The server checks a bound receipt before new-action phase/settlement guards. Exact retries, including after debrief, return the retained result. A changed actor, payload or group cannot reuse the receipt.

`serverState/candidates`, `serverState/candidateStationLiberations`, `candidateActionReceipts` and `candidateCalculationAudits` are server-owned. New mutations also create the shared private `actionAudits`/`procedureAudits` records once. Readable player documents contain only the minimal `candidateDisposition: {code, outcome, cycle}`. Detailed vessel disposition stays in GM-only root/server authority.

`commitTerminal` owns the immutable debrief and lifecycle transition in the candidate transaction. The candidate caller supplies a minimal public success marker, group evidence with audience labels, post-consequence survivor accounting and the allowed `candidateVesselOutcomes` patch. It does not write debrief state independently. Public success markers have only type, result, cause, candidate code, cycle and timestamp.

The shared player ship-action guard and `candidateAvailability` reject fresh source, target and host actions for settled candidate vessels. Standalone repair, mission, research, trade, detector and movement writers use the same private authority. An exact bound receipt returns before the fresh candidate guard, while live actor, GM instance, grant, role, seat, replacement, escape and custody checks remain required. Private `candidateCraftReservations` bind only a Ring contribution's craft, group, location, source request and cycle; operational player documents and generic member projections receive none of those fields. Optional admitted small-vessel authority uses its existing maintenance and host ledger; this group does not manufacture a new core crew seat or source profile.

The immutable ending receives structured path steps derived from committed candidate state: completed Ring repair, actual paid crossings and explicit sabotage; actual reached/lost Nebula attempts and their paid fuel; Station liberation, unique Reactor contributions and activated capacity. The steps use fixed action/result enums and scoped vessel IDs, fuel or capacity. They contain no coordinates, private request IDs, scouting arithmetic or free-text rule calls. The immutable debrief snapshot is the client authority for this explanation.
